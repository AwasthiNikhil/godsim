# Universe God Simulator: Validation Plan (v2)

* **Author:** Nikhil
* **Date:** 2026-11-09
* **Status:** Revised after the Goldfish review (`validation_plan_review.md`), then updated with the round-6 decisions (section 1.3). Effort numbers are planning ranges, not delivery commitments.
* **Builds on:** `concepts_brief.md` and brainstorm rounds 1 to 3.

## 0. Why this plan exists

### 0.1 The standard
> I create a universe, life emerges in unexpected ways, I discover and follow its history, understand why things happened, experiment with alternative timelines, and intervene whenever I feel like it.

### 0.2 What the plan must prove
These five priorities shape the plan. Every step below says which of them it proves (**P1** to **P5**).

| # | Priority |
|---|---|
| **P1** | Creatures evolve into visibly distinct, believable organisms. |
| **P2** | Their behaviour and survival are meaningfully tied to their bodies and environments. |
| **P3** | I can follow an individual and its lineage through generations. |
| **P4** | I can investigate why something happened and trace causes back to environmental conditions or my interventions. |
| **P5** | I can create an alternative timeline and discover how a small change affects the resulting history. |

Three of them are the **identity experiences**. They need player evidence early, before most of the engineering:

| Experience | Question | First tested |
|---|---|---|
| **Creature attachment** (P1, P3) | Does someone get interested in a generated creature and want to follow its lineage? | E2, E4 (around month 2 to 3) |
| **Causal discovery** (P4) | Is finding out *why* interesting, not just correct? | E1 (week 1 to 3), E2 |
| **Alternative timelines** (P5) | Can someone understand two histories after a small change, and get curious about the difference? | E1, E3 |

### 0.3 The long-term vision has not changed
A universe of evolving worlds, emergent life, civilisations, interstellar exploration and, eventually, civilisations that gain god-like powers of creation. The first build is smaller **in order to prove the experience**. It does not reduce the ambition. Every choice below is checked against one question: *does it rule out the larger vision?*

## 1. Decisions in force

### 1.1 Settled earlier (from `concepts_brief.md`)
- **Observation never changes history.** The camera, zoom, following and inspecting are view-only. Simulation detail is set per world.
- **Playback speed is view-only.** Fast-forward runs more fixed-size steps per real second. It never uses bigger steps or lower accuracy. If the machine can't keep up, that is a measured limit.
- **"Why?" contract.**
  - Recorded facts are checked first. Same-build replay is the fallback for unrecorded events in detailed history.
  - History from before detailed simulation is explained statistically.
  - Every answer is labelled Recorded, Reconstructed or Estimated.
  - Answers say honestly when a cause was chance or is uncertain. "Why not?" explains missing conditions or requirements.
- **Promotion.** By default the world switches to individual simulation automatically at a biological milestone. The player can also do it manually. Both are recorded. Promotion never invents individual history for the earlier era.
- **First build.** One star system and one living world. World size is set by measurement, so a limited ocean or an archipelago is acceptable.
- **Planning assumptions.** One developer, part-time (about 10 focused hours a week), no deadline, no funding.

### 1.2 Settled in this round
1. **The prologue is generated by rules, not scripted.**
   - The early era runs on simplified biological rules: a small number of lineages varying and being selected under the planet's conditions. They produce a few **founder organisms**.
   - Founder characteristics and diversity come out of those rules. No sequence of milestones is fixed in advance (no guaranteed "microbes, then multicellular, then swimmers"). Whatever the prologue reports is what actually happened in it.
   - The automatic promotion is a rule applied to the prologue's evolved state: the point where individual-level simulation becomes meaningfully valuable (see step P). It is not a step in a script.
   - Individual simulation starts from the founders. Their earlier history appears as the generated prologue, labelled Estimated, never as made-up individual ancestors.
2. **Replay stays in the first build, but is staged.**
   - The experience is tested first with saved simulation logs and explanations written by hand (the "Wizard-of-Oz" method: a person secretly supplies the answers). This tests the interface and whether "Why?" is interesting at all.
   - Recorded cause chains come next, then replay for Reconstructed answers.
   - The core experience is not validated until the game supplies genuine Recorded and Reconstructed answers. Hand-written answers are only a testing method, never the shipped system.
3. **The two-hour test is a first-build milestone.** It does not block starting the first playable prototype. It must pass before the core experience counts as validated.
4. **Art direction is decided after the silhouette and movement tests.** No commitment yet to 2D or 3D, realistic or stylised, or any particular look.
5. **Both openings are tested with real players.** Creation-first and discovery-first are compared, first on paper (E1), then in the playable build (S7a).

### 1.3 Settled in round 6 (2026-11-09)
1. **A recognisable predator–prey chase must be visible.** The minimum number of ticks this needs is measured in S0-lite, not fixed in advance. "Tick" and "generation" are defined in 2.1.
2. **View policies.** None of them may affect simulation history.
   - **Accepted:** at high playback speeds the view follows a **lineage**. Following one individual is a slower-speed mode.
   - **Accepted:** playback **slows down automatically at notable events**.
   - **Deferred:** running the simulation ahead of the display. It stays a possible later optimisation. It is not a first-build requirement.
3. **The engine is chosen right after G1**, based on what Phases A and B show. S0-lite is written with no dependency on any engine, so it can be ported.
4. **The prologue uses the same genome and body grammar as the detailed simulation.** It may use simplified biological rules and simplified rendering, but its founders must be valid genomes that move straight into detailed simulation without conversion.
5. **Testers.** Strangers are used for the important experience gates (G1, Playable 1, S7a, S7b). They are recruited from several groups, not only artificial-life communities: people interested in sandbox games, god games, evolution simulations and emergent worlds. Each tester's background is recorded, so that familiarity with the genre can be told apart from genuine interest in this game.
6. **Thresholds** in section 7 are initial hypotheses, with the changes listed there.
7. **Local law zones are deferred.** They stay in the long-term vision but are not a first-build requirement, because they don't prove any of P1 to P5.
8. **Promotion fires when individual simulation becomes meaningfully valuable,** not only at multicellularity (see step P). It must come from conditions that genuinely evolved. No milestone is ever scripted or forced to meet a time target.
9. **Effort numbers are planning ranges, not delivery commitments.** The 30% buffer may not be enough, especially for procedural movement and biological tuning.
10. **S7b behaviour target (provisional).**
    - At least 5 of 8 testers must independently show at least 4 of the 5 observed behaviours.
    - This is an **early continuation gate** (keep going or rethink). It is not statistical proof and not final validation.
    - Each behaviour is recorded separately, per tester, to show which parts of the experience attract players and which don't.
    - A behaviour counts only if it is **genuinely unprompted**. If a tester was told or nudged to follow, investigate, intervene or fork, that action is not evidence.
11. **People you know (provisional).** Allowed in E1, E2 and E5 as **formative evidence** only: useful for finding confusion and weaknesses, never counted toward passing a key gate. Gate evidence comes from strangers. See "Conventions" in section 3.

## 2. Corrected assumptions

### 2.1 Throughput: the previous estimate was about 10 times too optimistic
The previous plan said 10,000 generations at 20,000 creatures would take "10 minutes to a few hours". That figure needs about 8 nanoseconds per creature update, which is the cost of an update with no sensing at all. With neighbour sensing and cause tracking, one thread realistically manages **1 to 10 million updates per second**.

| Setup | Updates per generation | 10,000 generations at 1M/s | at 10M/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20k creatures, 365 ticks/generation | 7.3M | about 20 h | about 2 h |
| 20k creatures, 100 ticks/generation | 2M | about 5.5 h | about 33 min |
| 5k creatures, 100 ticks/generation | 0.5M | about 1.4 h | about 8 min |

**Definitions:**
- **Tick.** One simulation step. In one tick, every living creature senses, decides and acts once. Its movement is at most its speed in grid cells, and a fast creature takes several sub-steps, up to a fixed cap. Feeding, attacks, births and deaths resolve in a fixed order. Environment fields (light, tides, seasons) update on their own fixed schedule, for example every 10 ticks. "A day" is only a display label for a fixed number of ticks. A tick never changes size, so playback speed only changes how many ticks run per real second.
- **Generation time.** The average age, in ticks, of parents at the moment their offspring are born, measured over all births in a window. It is **measured, not set**, because it comes from evolved traits: maturity age, energy needed to reproduce, lifespan.
- **Generations elapsed** over a stretch of simulation = ticks in that stretch ÷ the generation time measured over it. For individual creatures, the generation count is also their lineage depth (how many parent-to-child steps from the founder). Both numbers are logged.
- **Creature update.** One creature's work in one tick. Throughput is creature updates per second, measured on the reference machine (section 7).

**Correction to the earlier wording:** ticks per generation cannot be set directly, because generation time evolves. What the design controls is (a) what one tick represents physically (how far a creature can move, how long an attack takes), and (b) the biological limits that shape generation time (minimum maturity age, energy cost of offspring). S0-lite measures the resulting generation time across seeds.

**Consequences:**
- **The chase sets the lower limit on ticks per lifetime.** A chase has to last enough ticks to be recognisable. S0-lite measures the minimum, rather than fixing it at 50 in advance.
- **Throughput then decides how fast evolution can run**, given that minimum.
- The first world will probably hold **thousands of creatures, not tens of thousands.** S0-lite measures the real figure.
- Pass targets are set from what the opening needs, not picked as free numbers.

### 2.2 You cannot watch one creature and watch evolution at the same speed
At evolution speed, one creature's life lasts a fraction of a second on screen. At a speed where you can watch an individual, evolution barely moves. Two view policies (decision 1.3.2) deal with this without breaking the view-only rule:
- **Follow lineages at speed.** The view tracks a family line and shows births, deaths and splits. Following one individual is a slow-speed mode.
- **Slow down at notable events.** Playback drops to watchable speed when the feed reports something (a split, an extinction, a first). The simulation itself is unchanged.

**Deferred: running ahead of the display.** Without it, fast-forward is limited to what the machine can compute in real time, and "skip to next event" runs the simulation at full speed until the event arrives. This is a known limit, not a hidden one. If S0-lite and Playable 1 show the waiting is a real problem, running ahead can be revisited.

### 2.3 Replay costs storage
A 3-second replay at about 5M updates/s covers about 750 ticks for a world of 20k creatures. That implies frequent checkpoints, and potentially gigabytes per 1,000 generations.

**Storage limits are provisional engineering targets, never a reason to lower the quality of history.** The facts record and cause links are never dropped to save space. If storage runs short, checkpoints thin out, which only makes replay of old events **slower**. It never makes answers wrong or vaguer. The order of priority is: correct history first, then storage, then replay speed. So:
- The **immediate cause of every death** is recorded compactly for a recent window. That answers most "Why did it die?" questions as Recorded, with no replay.
- **Replay** is for deeper or older questions. A progress bar is acceptable for old events.
- Checkpoints thin out with age.

### 2.4 "Won on the tidal flats" needs habitat attribution
For "Why?" to say *this lineage won because of the tidal flats your moon created*, the simulation has to record where each lineage's births happen and which habitats exist only because of a player setting. That is **habitat attribution**. It is cheap, but it has to be designed in from S0, along with tides and seasons as environment fields.

### 2.5 Statistical answers need to be interesting too
Answers about the prologue are Estimated, and they risk reading like a spreadsheet. The Price equation and selection gradients are established ways to split trait change into "selection" and "inheritance". They produce answers like "carriers survived cold 12% better". The label versions are tested in E1.

### 2.6 Spike code will become product code
The simulation core, save format, facts format and genome format written during the spikes will quietly become the product's formats. Each step below is marked **keep** or **throwaway**. Versioned formats are written before playtesters' saves start to matter.

## 3. The plan in four phases

### Conventions
- **Hours** are focused working hours. Calendar estimates assume **about 400 hours a year** (10 h/week over about 40 working weeks), which is about 33 hours a month.
- **[set]** thresholds have to be filled in before a step starts. Section 7 has proposed values.
- **Two classes of evidence (provisional, decision 1.3.11):**
  - **Gate evidence** decides whether a key experience gate passes (G1, Playable 1, S7a, S7b). It comes **only from strangers**, recruited from a mix of groups: people interested in sandbox games, god games, evolution simulations, emergent worlds and artificial life.
  - **Formative evidence** comes from people you know. It is allowed in E1, E2 and E5 so that recruiting doesn't delay the early experiments. It is used to find confusion, usability problems and obvious weaknesses. It **never counts toward passing a key gate**.
  - Every result is tagged *gate* or *formative* in its write-up.
  - **E3 and E4 use stranger judges.** These tests depend on independent judgements (which timeline changed, grouping creature silhouettes, rating movement), and familiarity with the author or the project could bias them.
- The author never judges experience criteria. Each tester's background is recorded (genres they play, familiarity with evolution simulations), so results can be split by it.
- Effort numbers are **planning ranges, not delivery commitments**.
- Every step ends with a short write-up: pass, fail, or "pass with a changed assumption".

### Phase A: Experience on paper (no code) — weeks 1 to 3

#### E0. Baseline on existing games
- **Proves:** sets the bar for all priorities. Tests the screensaver risk.
- **Do:**
  - Play The Bibites and Species: ALRE for about 2 hours each.
  - Watch 2 or 3 people play one of them for 30 minutes.
  - Note when attention drops, what they wish they could ask, and what they would want to follow.
- **Output:** a list of the moments where attention drops and the questions players asked. This sets S7's bar.
- **Effort:** 3–6 h. **Throwaway.**

#### E1. Paper session: causal discovery, timelines, opening A/B
- **Proves:** P4, P5, the opening, and the "Why?" labels.
- **Do:**
  - You act as the simulation.
  - **Creation arm:** the tester chooses moon, tilt and water from cards. You then reveal the matching pre-prepared outcome (four outcomes: moon yes/no × water low/high).
  - **Discovery arm:** the tester gets the same outcome with no choice.
  - Hand over feed cards, a "Why?" chain in three label versions (all Recorded; mixed; Estimated only), and a hand-drawn comparison of two timelines.
- **Pass:**
  - At least 3 of 5 testers per arm ask a follow-up "why" without being prompted.
  - Testers can explain the labels in their own words.
  - At least half say what changed between the two timelines and want to know why.
  - Record which arm produced more ownership and curiosity. This is evidence, not a final verdict.
- **Fail → reconsider:** if causal chains bore testers on paper, rethink how "Why?" is presented before building any of it.
- **Effort:** 8–12 h. **Throwaway.**

#### E5. Promotion presentation mock-ups
- **Proves:** P3 (lineages that reach "history starts here"), and that the honest presentation is understood.
- **Do:** Show testers three mock-ups of the boundary where a lineage reaches the founders:
  - a fog line;
  - a "Founders" badge;
  - a "river" of population narrowing into individuals.

  Ask: "What happened before this?"
- **Pass:** most testers read it as "earlier history, known less precisely", not as missing data or a bug.
- **Effort:** 3–5 h (can run alongside E1). **Throwaway.**

**Phase A total: 14–23 h. First player evidence by week 1 to 3.**

### Phase B: Experience on real simulation data — about months 1 to 4

#### S0-lite. Minimal core simulation
- **Proves:** P2 (foundation), and supplies real data for E2 to E4.
- **Build:**
  - A 2D grid world with sunlight, nutrients, terrain, and a **tide field** (wet/dry band) and **seasons** as environment fields.
  - Creatures with trait genomes that move by ticks, sense their neighbours, eat, reproduce with mutation and die. Energy is lost at each step up the food chain.
  - Built for determinism from day one:
    - all simulation state in **integers**;
    - a **counter-based random number source** keyed on (seed, creature, tick, purpose);
    - a **state hash** every N ticks.
  - A **timing harness** that measures updates per second for sensing, movement and logging separately (this replaces the old S3a).
  - A **ground-truth cause log** (every death with its cause) and **habitat records** (where births happen).
  - Interventions recorded as logged inputs.
  - Generation time and generations elapsed are logged using the definitions in 2.1.
  - **No dependency on any game engine.** It is plain, headless code with its own data structures, so it can be ported or connected to whichever engine is chosen after G1.
- **Chase measurement** (decision 1.3.1):
  - Using the throwaway viewer from E2, play back predator–prey encounters with a tick representing different physical scales (different movement distances per tick).
  - 3 viewers who don't know the setting say whether each clip shows "a chase" or "creatures just appearing next to each other".
  - Record the **minimum ticks per encounter** and **minimum ticks per lifetime** at which at least 2 of 3 see a chase.
  - That figure, combined with the measured throughput, sets the world size and evolution speed for the opening.
- **Pass (provisional):**
  - At least 8 of 10 seeds survive 2,000 generations with at least 5 trait clusters.
  - Two runs give identical hashes.
  - Measured throughput is recorded for 2k, 5k and 20k creatures, and the measured generation time is recorded for each.
  - A minimum chase length has been found.
- **Fail → reconsider:**
  - If ecosystems collapse, add habitat variety and refuges, never balancing code.
  - If throughput is far below 1M/s, shrink the world.
  - If no tick scale gives a recognisable chase at usable throughput, chases become a slow-speed view: something you see only when following an individual. That needs your decision (section 8).
- **Effort:** 20–35 h. **Keep** (becomes the core).

#### E2. Replayed-history viewer with hand-answered "Why?"
- **Proves:** **creature attachment** (P3) and **causal discovery** (P4) on real emergent data.
- **Build:** a non-interactive viewer (a quick browser canvas is enough) that plays back S0-lite logs. It shows:
  - lineage colours;
  - a text feed;
  - a growing tree of life;
  - one creature's life history.

  You answer "Why?" live from the log, using the three labels.
- **Test:** 3 to 5 testers, 20 minutes each.
- **Pass:**
  - At least 3 of 5 pick a creature or lineage and ask what happened to it next.
  - At least 3 of 5 ask a "why" question you can answer from the log, and rate at least one answer as interesting.
  - Record **which questions people actually ask**. This decides which cause types S2 must record.
  - Record **whether "following" means watching live or reading a life history**. This decides the follow view.
- **Fail → reconsider:** if nobody cares about any creature on real data, the problem is the creatures or the presentation, not the engineering. Go to E4 findings before building more.
- **Effort:** 12–20 h. **Throwaway** viewer; the findings are kept.

#### E3. Paper fork on real data
- **Proves:** **alternative timelines** (P5).
- **Do:**
  - Run S0-lite with tides on and off over 5 to 10 seeds.
  - Print the trees of life side by side, with a hand-written list of the top 3 differences.
  - Testers who don't know which side was changed say what differs and whether they care.
- **Pass:** in at least 7 of 10 pairs, judges who don't know which side changed find the meaningful difference. At least half of the testers want to know *why* it changed.
- **Also measures:** whether a single change gives broad differences that repeat across seeds (the player can learn) while details still vary.
- **Fail → reconsider:** if tides on/off make no visible difference, the environment's effect on life is too weak. That is a P2 problem, so fix it in S0, not in the fork interface.
- **Effort:** 4–8 h. **Throwaway.**

#### E4 (S4a). Creature silhouettes and body-to-ability mapping
- **Proves:** **creature attachment** (P1) and P2.
- **Build:**
  - A first body grammar (6 to 8 module functions with repeat and mirror rules) drawn as **static silhouettes in one uniform colour**.
  - A mapping from body to ability, plugged into S0-lite: mouth size decides prey size, limb length decides speed and energy cost, and so on.
  - Also show testers clips from existing creature games, to find the bar for movement quality that they actually expect.
- **Pass:**
  - Blind grouping of 30 creatures from 3 lineages (all from the same environment): at least 4 of 5 raters reach an adjusted Rand index of 0.5 or more. This is a standard measure of how well their groups match the true lineages.
  - Asked "which would you follow, and why?", testers give reasons based on body shape or behaviour.
  - Body traits measurably change survival in S0-lite.
- **Fail → reconsider:** if creatures look alike, add larger structural mutations (duplicating segments) and wider environment differences, not more module types. If that still fails, choose a stylised direction where behaviour carries the variety.
- **Effort:** 15–25 h. The grammar is **kept**; the renderer is **throwaway**.

#### G1. Experience gate (keep going or stop)
- **Proves:** all three identity experiences together, before the heavy engineering.
- **Do:** a 30-minute session on E2 and E3 material plus E4 creatures, with 3 testers who are not friends.
- **Pass:** at least 2 of 3 ask, without being prompted, to see what happens next.
- **Fail → reconsider:** revisit the core loop (mysteries, event-driven time, discovery-first opening) **before** building S1 onward. Do not add layers to fix a failing core.
- **Effort:** 4–8 h.

**Phase B total: 55–96 h. Cumulative: about 69–119 h, roughly months 2 to 4. A keep-going-or-stop decision on the experience is made here.**

### Phase C: First playable prototype — about months 4 to 9

The goal is **something playable as early as possible**. It has a live world, following, a feed, a "Why?" built on recorded causes, and forks, before every technical question is settled.

#### Engine decision
- **Do:** right after G1, choose the engine or framework based on what Phases A and B showed: whether 2D or 3D is more readable, what the viewer needs, and the measured throughput. Connect the engine-independent S0-lite core, or port it if the language changes. The engine handles display and input only. The simulation core stays separate from it.
- **Effort:** 20–40 h. **Keep.**

#### S1. Determinism, the remaining work
- **Proves:** foundation for P4 and P5.
- **Questions:**
  - Are hashes unchanged by save/reload, pausing, playback speed, automatic slow-down and lineage-following?
  - Do they match on a second machine (your Apple Silicon Mac and one x86-64 PC)?
  - Do they still match after the port into the chosen engine?
- **Pass:**
  - Identical hashes over 1 million ticks at about 2,000 creatures, under random viewer actions, and on both machines.
  - The actual runtime of that run is recorded on each machine.
- **Fail → reconsider:** a viewer that changes results is a bug and must be fixed. A mismatch between machines means accepting same-platform replay and saying so.
- **Deferred:** determinism across thread counts.
- **Effort:** 6–12 h.

#### S2-core. Facts record, recorded causes, forks
- **Proves:** P4 and P5 with real answers instead of hand-written ones.
- **Build:**
  - A facts log with cause links (SQLite is enough).
  - The recent-window cause record from 2.3.
  - Cause chains that reach environmental conditions and player actions.
  - Habitat attribution.
  - "Why not?" from failed requirement checks.
  - Checkpoints (compressed snapshots that thin out with age).
  - Forking from any checkpoint.
  - A build ID in every save.
- **Three separate measures.** They are kept apart on purpose, so that an interesting answer can never make up for a wrong one.
  1. **Validity (must pass).**
     - Every answer matches the ground-truth log. This is a regression check that causes are recorded correctly and that cause tracking never uses a random draw.
     - When the true cause is chance or old age, the answer says so.
     - No answer contains a cause that isn't in the record or the replay.
     - **There is no target for how often answers may be "chance" or "old age".** The system must never invent causes to meet a percentage.
  2. **Usefulness (diagnostic, not a pass/fail target).**
     - Share of extinctions whose cause chain reaches an environmental condition or player action within 4 links.
     - Share of answers that are "chance" or "old age".
     - Average chain length.

     These numbers say whether the *simulation* produces causes worth discovering. If they're poor, the fix is richer simulation (more varied environments and pressures, more cause types informed by E2), never different answer wording.
  3. **Interest (tester-rated).** 3 raters who are not the author rate 20 answers for "learned something" and "wanted to ask a follow-up". Proposed initial target: at least 60% "learned something".
- **Engineering targets (provisional):**
  - Checkpoints and facts stay within the storage targets in section 7. If they don't, the targets are revisited. History quality is never lowered to meet them (see 2.3).
  - A fork starts in 2 seconds or less, using at most twice one world's memory.
- **Fail → reconsider:** if answers are valid but rarely useful or interesting, the world isn't producing enough discoverable causes. Look at environment variety and selection pressure first.
- **Effort:** 30–50 h. **Keep.**

#### Playable 1 (an experience prototype)
- **What it is:** an **experience prototype**, built to find out whether the identity experiences survive being interactive. It is **not** a complete game, and passing it does **not** validate the core experience. That happens only at the S7b milestone.
- **Proves:** early evidence on P2 to P5 together, interactively, for the first time.
- **Build:**
  - The world running live.
  - Lineage-following and individual-following.
  - Feed and tripwires.
  - A tree of life.
  - A "Why?" panel answering from **recorded causes** (Reconstructed answers come in Phase D).
  - Forks shown side by side.
  - 4 or 5 interventions.
  - Automatic slow-down at events.

  Creatures use E4 silhouettes with simple movement.
- **Test:** 3 to 5 testers, 30 minutes each. Repeat E2's questions on the real thing.
- **Pass:** at least as good as E2 on attachment and causal-discovery questions. If it's worse, the interactive version lost something, and the cause needs finding.
- **Effort:** 30–60 h. **Keep.**

**Phase C total: 86–162 h. Cumulative: about 155–281 h, roughly months 5 to 9 (6 to 11 with buffer). This is the first playable experience prototype, not a validated game.**

### Phase D: Complete and validate the first build — about months 9 to 22

All of these are **essential** for the core experience to count as validated, but each one improves a game that is already playable.

| Step | What it does | Proves | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 tuning + species detection** | Proper species detection (genome distance), diversity floor, tree of life from detected species | P1, P2 | 20–35 h |
| **S5 Ecosystems and diversity** | 10–20 seeds × 3 setups, runs capped at about 2,000 generations. Headless moon on/off test. Perception check: people match body sets to their environment. | P2, P4 | 25–45 h |
| **S4b Movement** | Procedural gaits, starting from one movement family (for example swimming or soft-bodied) chosen from E4 findings. Re-run S5 with full bodies. **Only if E4 passed.** | P1, P2 | 40–100 h |
| **W Planet model** | Simple climate by latitude, tides from moon mass, seasons from tilt, live "where life could start" overlays for creation | Opening, P4 | 20–40 h |
| **P Procedural prologue** | A few lineages evolve under simplified rules, using **the same genome and body grammar** as the detailed simulation, under the planet's conditions. Founders and promotion emerge from it. Estimated answers come from selection statistics. | P3, P4, opening | 15–30 h |
| **S2-replay** | Replay from checkpoints for Reconstructed answers to unrecorded events; version guard | P4 | 15–25 h |
| **S6-lite Fork comparison** | Automatic ranking of meaningful differences between timelines; cost of running two worlds | P5 | 10–20 h |
| **S7a Opening test** | Both openings in the real build, 4 testers each | Opening, all | 20–40 h |
| **S7b Two-hour milestone** | 8 testers who are not friends; take-home build | All | 40–80 h |

**S5 pass criteria:**
- At least 70% of seeds keep 3 or more food-chain levels for the capped run.
- The difference between setups is at least 2 times the difference between seeds within one setup.
- In the moon on/off test, at least 60% of seeds show a top-ranked difference in tidal habitat.
- Novelty rate (changes in body-plan clusters) in the last 20% of the run is at least 10% of the rate in the first 20%.
- In the perception check, 3 people match body sets to environments with at least 70% accuracy.

**S4b pass criteria:**
- On randomly sampled clips rated by people other than the author, at least 60% score 3 or more out of 5, and at most 20% are called "broken".
- "Nonsense" bodies make up less than 10% of creatures after selection. Nonsense means parts that intersect, or speed under 5% of the median for that body size.

**P: when does promotion fire?**
Promotion fires when **individual-level simulation becomes meaningfully valuable**: when the differences between individuals start deciding outcomes in ways that population averages can't capture. Multicellularity is one route to that, not the only one. Candidate triggers, all checked against the prologue's evolved state:
- at least one lineage has bodies with more than one module function beyond the core (a body plan with parts);
- at least one lineage moves under its own power and interacts with other lineages (predation, competition for the same food);
- a lineage has formed colonies or aggregates whose arrangement affects survival.

The step P spike tests these and keeps whichever rule (or combination) produces worthwhile individual-level worlds. The rule is evaluated only on evolved state. **No milestone is ever scripted or forced to meet a time target.** If promotion is too slow, the allowed fixes are the default planet settings and the prologue's simplified rules, not forcing.

**P pass criteria:**
- Founders are valid genomes that move straight into detailed simulation, and they differ in ways traceable to the planet's conditions.
- **Promotion timing:** across 50 seeds of the default world, promotion fires within **6 real minutes on at least 90% of seeds**. 3 minutes is a stretch target. The **median and worst-case** times are recorded, and seeds where promotion never fires are reported separately.
- Automatic and manual promotion at the same step give the same state hash.
- Testers understand the boundary (from the E5 winner).

**Opening timeline measurements** (recorded in P, Playable 1, S7a and S7b; promotion time alone doesn't show an engaging opening):

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Time to first visible life | Real time from start until anything alive is shown on screen (for example, mats at a vent or shore) |
| Time to first interesting biological event | Real time until the first feed event of a notable type (a lineage split, an extinction, a first predation, a body-plan first) |
| Time to promotion | Real time until individual simulation starts |
| Time to first follow opportunity | Real time until the player can follow a specific individual or lineage |

These are measured, with median and worst case, across seeds and testers. Targets are set after Playable 1, once there is real data on where attention drops.

**S7a pass criteria:**
- In at least half of the sessions, the first "Why?" in the first 30 minutes traces back to the tester's own choice, **with weight**: the habitat or selection share is above the section 7 value, or a fork without that choice confirms it.
- Testers rate the link as non-obvious or satisfying.
- The four opening timeline measures are recorded for both openings.
- Record which opening wins.

**S7b criteria (the milestone that validates the core experience):**

*Measured outcomes:*
- At least 5 of 8 play past 60 minutes.
- At least 3 of 8 reopen the take-home build unprompted within a week.

*Observed behaviours* (recorded by an observer or session log; each one counts only if it happens **without a prompt**):
- **Follows:** picks an individual or lineage and follows it for a meaningful stretch.
- **Gets interested in histories:** looks at a creature's ancestry or a lineage's past, or names a lineage.
- **Investigates:** asks "Why?" or "Why not?" about something they noticed, and follows up on the answer.
- **Intervenes:** changes the world on purpose.
- **Wants the consequences:** after intervening, watches or forks to see what the intervention caused.

*Comprehension and story:*
- Every tester can retell a story that includes a cause ("X died out because Y").
- "Why?" answers are rated interesting, not just used often.
- Testers can explain a chain with mixed labels unprompted.
- No tester describes the promotion boundary as missing or broken data.

*Analysis:* results are split by tester background, to separate genre familiarity from interest in this game. All 8 testers are strangers (gate evidence).

**Provisional target (decision 1.3.10):** at least 5 of 8 testers independently show at least 4 of the 5 observed behaviours. This is an **early continuation gate**, not statistical proof or final validation.

**Recording rules:**
- Each behaviour is logged separately for each tester, as a tester × behaviour grid, with the time it first happened and what triggered it.
- The session script contains **no instructions or hints** to follow, investigate, intervene or fork. Testers get only the basic controls.
- If an observer or the interface prompted a behaviour (a tutorial step, a suggestion, a question from the observer), that occurrence is logged as *prompted* and does not count.
- Interface suggestions that are part of the game itself (for example, the feed offering "Why?") are allowed, but they are recorded, so their influence can be judged.

**Phase D total: 205–415 h. Cumulative: about 360–696 h before buffer.**

### 5.2 Hardest parts for one developer

## 8. Remaining decisions and tensions

The round-6 decisions are recorded in 1.3 and section 7. These items are still open:

1. **If chases need more ticks than throughput allows.** S0-lite may find that a recognisable chase needs so many ticks per lifetime that evolution in the opening becomes too slow. The choice then is between:
   - (a) a smaller world;
   - (b) a slower opening;
   - (c) chases visible only when following an individual at slow speed.

   This is decided when the measurement exists, not now.
2. **Promotion within 6 minutes vs. "never forced".** These can conflict. If the default world misses the 6-minute target, the only allowed fixes are the default planet settings and the prologue's simplified rules. If those can't reach 90%, the choice is to relax the target or accept a longer opening. Forcing the milestone is not an option.
3. **Which promotion trigger** (body plan, mobility and interaction, colonies, or a combination). The P spike tests them. The final choice is yours once the results are in.

All three wait on data. No decision is needed before Phase A starts.

**Tension to watch:** the feed suggesting "Why?" is part of the game, but it nudges players toward investigating. S7b records, for every investigation, whether the feed suggested it. Investigating because something caught the player's attention is different from following a suggestion. Look at that evidence before deciding whether the feed is helping a working curiosity loop or covering for a weak one.
